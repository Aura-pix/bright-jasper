Everybody's talking about the Dangote Refinery IPO right now. My WhatsApp is full of bamboo links. Guides, tutorials, "how to invest" posts everywhere — and honestly, good. It means people believe in something local for once.

Then Aliko Dangote himself went on [ARISE News](https://www.arise.tv/dangote-%E2%82%A6100000-refinery-investment-could-make-you-a-millionaire-within-two-years/) with Ojy Okpe, days before the offer opened, and said the quiet part out loud. Shares are going for ₦525 right now. He thinks they could hit ₦10,000.

> "If you invest like something like maybe ₦100,000, within one year, two years or so, you're talking about you becoming a millionaire."

He called it "a future for anybody's family." He also said dividends could come in dollars. Nice pitch. We'll get to how much of that the [actual prospectus](https://ipo.dangote.com/prospectus.pdf) backs up, and how much is just a good interview.

The offer opened Monday, September 14 — first time in NGX's 66-year history a company has listed this way, gong and all. ₦2.15 trillion raise. It crashed a popular investment app within hours of opening. Two days before it even opened, [₦1.9 trillion left the NGX](https://www.insideojodu.com/dangote-refinery-ipo-ngx-loses-massive-n1-9tn-in-two-days-as-investors-sell-stocks/) as other investors sold off to free up cash for this one — which tells you a chunk of this "new" money is really just old money changing seats.

Femi Otedola's the loudest name doing exactly that. He sold his majority stake in Geregu Power — [reportedly a $750 million deal](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/business/business-news/882091-why-i-sold-ownership-of-geregu-power-plant-otedola.html) — and personally asked Dangote to reserve him $100 million in refinery shares, after visiting the site 25 times. Hold that number. We're coming back to it.

## How much is Dangote Refinery worth?

Here's the thing people aren't talking about: **Dangote Refinery is already priced like it's the best refinery on the planet.**

The offer values it at **$47–49 billion**. It cost about **[$19–20 billion to build](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/1109-58813-dangotes-refinery-seeks-1-6-billion-for-just-3-3-of-its-shares-a-tenth-of-its-expansion-bill)**. The market wants you to pay two and a half times what it took to construct, before it's even proven a full year of stable profit.

Compare it to [Marathon Petroleum](https://robinhood.com/us/en/stocks/MPC/) — America's biggest refiner, 13 plants, 3 million barrels a day — trading at $116 billion. Or [Tüpraş](https://companiesmarketcap.com/turkiye-petrol-rafinerileri/marketcap/), Turkey's national refiner, four refineries, at $11.5 billion. Per barrel of capacity, Dangote is already pricier than both. One refinery, in one country, is already commanding a premium over refiners twice and ten times its size.

## What would ₦10,000 require?

₦525 to ₦10,000 is roughly **19x**. That's about **$1 trillion in valuation**.

At the kind of per-barrel price the market pays for Marathon Petroleum, Dangote would need to control about a quarter of the entire planet's refining capacity — one company, 25 million of the world's roughly 104 million barrels a day (per the Energy Institute's global refining data). At Tüpraş's price, closer to half the planet.

Or keep Dangote's own current premium price and ask what capacity gets you there: about **14 million barrels a day**. Ten times what the company has ever said it's building.

None of these are real valuation models — margins and growth matter more than raw barrels. But as a gut check on what a ₦10,000 refinery would need to physically look like, the answer is: **bigger than anything that has ever existed.**

One honest comparison: [Saudi Aramco](https://companiesmarketcap.com/saudi-aramco/marketcap/) actually did [IPO near $1.7 trillion](https://gulfnews.com/business/aramco-share-price-range-30-32-saudi-riyals-1.1573974002608) without achieving a 5x. Aramco isn't really a refiner though — it's the world's biggest oil producer with refining on top. It proves a trillion-dollar energy IPO can happen. It doesn't prove this one can get there.

## What market would it need to own?

60% of the refinery's crude still comes from Nigeria on term contracts with NNPC, capped around **350,000 barrels a day** (per the offer prospectus). The rest is imported, competing on the open market like everyone else. To get anywhere near the capacity numbers above, Dangote would need crude supply at a scale that simply doesn't exist as spare capacity in West Africa right now.

Meanwhile, Nigeria's legacy refineries — Port Harcourt, Warri, and Kaduna — are being rehabilitated. If they return to service, they could add competing domestic capacity in the coming years. That's a headwind, even if it emerges slowly.

## What are you actually buying?

Quick reality checks before you fund that trading wallet:

1. **You're buying the refinery.** Not Dangote Cement, not the whole Dangote Group, not Aliko Dangote's other businesses. One asset.
2. **Dangote personally controls ~87–89%.** The public gets roughly 3–4%, and the prospectus allows the company to sell up to 30% more shares if demand is strong — which [looks like exactly what's happening](https://www.billionaires.africa/2026/09/17/aliko-dangotes-refinery-ipo-could-add-60-billion-to-nigerias-stock-market/). The ownership picture could shift meaningfully by the time the offer closes on October 13.
3. **Two regulatory clocks are ticking:** the naira-for-crude arrangement expires July 2027. The tax exemption on domestic sales ends January 2028. Both sit well inside any "hold it for a few years" plan.
4. **Oil is expensive right now** — [above $100 a barrel](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil), mostly because of Middle East supply disruptions. That's flattering today's numbers, which is by nature temporary. Brent was near $60 less than a year ago.

## Who wins at these numbers?

Here's why Otedola's $100 million matters more. If this stock does something completely realistic — **2x, maybe 5x over several years** — that's an excellent outcome for someone putting in $100 million. It's also excellent for someone putting in ₦100,000.

![Charlie Munger quote about average investors buying IPOs](/munger.png)

Neither of them necessarily needs ₦10,000 a share to win. Institutional money repositioning around this IPO is underwriting a 2–5x story. It's the retail pitch reaching for the extra zero to feel the essence of the investment.

## The club that reaches $1 trillion valuation

Apple took 13 years to go from $50 billion to $1 trillion. Amazon did it in under a decade. Nvidia did it almost overnight, riding a demand wave nobody predicted.

What they share is bigger than growth — each one created or completely owned a **new category**. Oil refining is a hundred-year-old, thin-margin, commodity business. Nothing wrong with that, it's just a different kind of story.

## The long game nobody's pricing in

![Meme about energy investors waiting for oil to make a big comeback](/escober.png)

The [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/world-energy-outlook-2025/executive-summary) expects global oil demand to peak around **2030** and slowly decline after that, as EVs take over and solar and nuclear keep growing fast. That's one scenario, not a certainty.

What's fairly consistent across scenarios: **petrochemicals, aviation and trucking keep growing** even as passenger fuel demand falls. Not that refineries are dying or crude is going obsolete, but the need for it gets a little lower every year the world electrifies further.

## What's realistic?

A well-run refinery in a market that still imports fuel, growing **2–5x over years, maybe decades**. Nobody should call that a consolation prize. For patient money, that's a genuinely strong return.

On dividends: Dangote has said publicly you could be paid in dollars. Real hedge against the naira, if it holds. But the prospectus itself is far more careful — dividends are entirely at the board's discretion, subject to profit, cash flow, and "financial covenants" tied to the company's loans. The dollar-dividend dream is real intent, but not a guarantee. Go read the actual document — the interview alone won't tell you that part.

## Who owns Dangote Refinery?

This part doesn't get talked about enough. Straight from the prospectus:

Four names hold the company pre-listing: **Dangote Oil Refining Company (65.8%)**, **Dangote Industries (14.9%)**, **Greenview International (6.5%)**, and **NNPC (6.8%)**. Looks like four owners.

**It's really one.** The prospectus confirms Aliko Dangote personally beneficially owns the first three, plus a majority stake in a fourth vehicle. Add it up: **about 87% of the company, one man, spread across paper.** The public — what this IPO is actually selling you — starts at roughly 3–4%, before the 30% expansion clause kicks in.

## Why this IPO still matters anyway

None of this is a shot at the project or at Dangote himself.

Forget the share price target for a second. This single listing could add **$60 billion to the entire Nigerian Exchange**. A ₦2.15 trillion offer chose Lagos over London or New York — a real bet on Nigerian capital markets, and one that could open the door for other big companies to list at home instead of abroad. And this is a refinery supplying close to **80% of Nigeria's petrol**, exporting the rest across West Africa, cutting a dependency that's cost the country for decades.

There's also something to Dangote's whole playbook — cement, sugar, now fuel — always controlling the full chain end to end instead of one link of it. Whatever you think of ₦10,000, that's a genuinely different way to build industrial scale in Africa, and it's part of why this thing got built at all.

Here's the reverberation I believe we should be watching: **less fuel imported, more Nigerian savings going into Nigerian assets instead of sitting offshore**, and if this works, other African industrialists start copying the template. Slower than "₦100,000 becomes a million," sure. But it could move the whole continent, long after nobody remembers what the ticker said in year one.

## Where I land

I don't think Dangote is being delusional. Delusion, in the right dose, is probably what it takes to build Africa's biggest refinery when everyone said it couldn't be done at that scale, in that location, on that timeline. And I don't think he set out to mislead 10 million ordinary Nigerians on an offer that was already oversubscribed within hours of opening anyway.

But here's where I actually land, numbers and all: **₦10,000 — a roughly 20x move to something near a trillion-dollar valuation — is extremely unlikely on anything this refinery has ever said it's building.** A **2x to 5x move**, putting the company somewhere in the **$95–240 billion range**, is a realistic outcome over the next several years, and the odds of landing on the higher end of that improve if Dangote holds onto its current crude allocation, the 1.4 million bpd expansion lands close to schedule, and the dollar-dividend promise survives contact with the covenants actually written into the prospectus. Retail investors buying at ₦525 for that outcome aren't being scammed. They're just being sold a headline that belongs a few zeros lower than the one making the rounds.

For now, we just go back to our regular lives and watch one of the continent's boldest risk-takers try, again, to make the unrealistic realistic. I'm rooting for the refinery. Just not for the ₦10,000.
