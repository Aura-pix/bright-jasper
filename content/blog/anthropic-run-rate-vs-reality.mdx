I started digging into Anthropic because I kept seeing the same two facts side by side, and they didn't seem to fit together.

Fact one: Anthropic's revenue "run rate" hit $65 billion by the end of July 2026.

Fact two: the company was worth $61.5 billion, total, fourteen months earlier.

Something in that gap felt like it needed unpacking before I could decide what any of it actually meant. So I went looking for what "run rate" means in practice, what's confirmed versus estimated, and what Anthropic actually is underneath the valuation headlines.

Here's what I found.

## What Anthropic actually is

Skip the "AI chatbot company" framing. That's the least useful way to describe what's happening here.

Anthropic makes the Claude family of models, but the business isn't really selling chat access to consumers. It's an enterprise infrastructure company. Businesses build on Claude through the API, run agentic coding through Claude Code, and increasingly deploy Claude inside their own internal tools and workflows. The company has said its customer base includes over 300,000 businesses, with enterprise accounts making up roughly 80% of total sales.

That distinction matters, because it explains the second thing that makes Anthropic unusual: how fast the money is moving.

## The valuation run-up, in order

Anthropic's private valuation history reads like a chart with the wrong scale on the y-axis.

March 2025: $61.5 billion, after a $3.5 billion round led by Lightspeed Venture Partners.

September 2025: $183 billion.

February 2026: $380 billion, a $30 billion Series G round.

May 2026: $965 billion, a $65 billion Series H round led by Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital, among others — reportedly the company's last private round before going public.

That's a roughly 15x increase in fourteen months. Private markets don't usually move like that. When they do, it's almost always because revenue is moving even faster, and investors are pricing in where that revenue is headed rather than where it already is.

Which brings me to the number that actually needs unpacking.

## The $65 billion isn't what it sounds like

Here's the distinction that matters most in this whole story, and it's the one most headlines skip.

"Run-rate revenue" is not annual revenue. It's a projection: take your most recent month (or quarter) of revenue and multiply it out to a full year, as if that pace holds for twelve straight months. It's a legitimate way to communicate momentum. It is not the same thing as money the company has actually collected over a calendar year.

Anthropic's disclosed run-rate trajectory through 2026 looked like this: roughly $9 billion at the end of 2025, $14 billion in February, $19 billion in March, $30 billion in April, $47 billion in May (announced alongside the Series H), and $65 billion by the end of July.

Actual quarterly revenue for the most recent completed quarter was reported at just over $11.5 billion — up sharply from $787 million in the same quarter a year earlier, which is a real and extraordinary growth rate. But annualizing a single strong month or quarter and calling it "revenue" produces a bigger number than what actually lands on the income statement for the year.

One analysis I came across, comparing Anthropic's monthly run-rate disclosures against how the metric compounds, estimated that Anthropic's *actual* calendar-2026 revenue would likely land somewhere around $20–26 billion — a fraction of the $65 billion figure making headlines. I want to be careful here: that specific range is an outside estimate, not a number Anthropic has confirmed, and it should be treated as an informed calculation rather than fact. But the underlying mechanical point stands regardless of whose estimate you use: a run-rate figure and a full-year revenue figure answer different questions, and conflating them is where a lot of the "Anthropic is already bigger than Salesforce" framing falls apart.

The honest version: Anthropic's revenue is growing at a pace with very few precedents in software history. It is not yet the equivalent of the biggest software companies on earth, no matter how the run-rate number gets described in a press release.

## Where the growth is actually coming from

The clearest single driver is Claude Code, Anthropic's agentic coding product. Its run-rate went from roughly $500 million in September 2025 to around $8 billion by May 2026 — and third-party trackers (not confirmed by Anthropic) put it near $15 billion by August. Independent analysis has attributed a majority share of coding-specific AI spend to Claude Code specifically.

The second driver is enterprise concentration. Anthropic has said the number of customers spending more than $1 million annually roughly doubled between February and mid-2026, from around 500 to over 1,000. That's a meaningfully different growth shape than a consumer subscription business — a small number of very large enterprise contracts, rather than millions of small individual ones. It's also a concentration risk, which I'll come back to.

## The infrastructure bet underneath the software story

This is the part that makes Anthropic genuinely unusual to analyze, and it's the part a pure "software company" lens misses.

To keep training and serving frontier models, Anthropic has committed to an enormous amount of compute capacity: reported commitments include up to 5 gigawatts with Amazon, multiple gigawatts with Google and Broadcom, roughly $30 billion of Azure cloud capacity, a $50 billion U.S. infrastructure partnership with Fluidstack, and further capacity from SpaceX. To backstop those commitments and reduce pressure to time an IPO around cash needs, Anthropic reportedly arranged a $15 billion revolving credit facility ahead of its expected public listing.

That's the tension at the center of the whole company: software-style revenue growth, running on top of infrastructure-style capital commitments. If revenue keeps compounding faster than the compute bill, the model works beautifully — Anthropic's estimated 2026 losses (roughly $2 billion, by one outside analyst's unaudited estimate) are reportedly a fraction of rival OpenAI's projected losses for the same year. If growth slows while those multi-year compute contracts are still locked in, the economics get much less comfortable, much faster.

## The real risks, not manufactured ones

I don't think Anthropic needs an invented bear case any more than Dangote did. There's a real one already sitting in the disclosed facts.

**Its two biggest partners are also its rivals.** Amazon and Google are both major investors in Anthropic and its primary compute suppliers — and both companies also build and sell their own competing model franchises. That's a structural conflict most software companies simply don't have: a supplier who benefits if you fail.

**The IPO disclosures haven't happened yet.** Anthropic confidentially filed for its IPO on June 1, 2026, and is reportedly working with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan. Until the S-1 becomes public, GAAP revenue, gross margin, customer concentration and actual cash burn are not independently verifiable — everything currently circulating is either company-disclosed run-rate data or outside estimates. Exact IPO timing and pricing figures currently circulating (some outlets suggest a mid-to-late October 2026 window) are forecasts, not confirmed dates.

**Regulatory exposure is not theoretical — it already happened.** In June 2026, Anthropic had to suspend access to two of its newer models, Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5, in response to a U.S. Department of Commerce export control directive. Access was restored about three weeks later, after the relevant controls were lifted. That's a concrete, disclosed example of how a policy decision outside the company's control can interrupt product availability overnight — a genuine risk factor for any frontier AI company operating under close government scrutiny, not a hypothetical one.

**Competition isn't standing still.** OpenAI, Anthropic's closest rival, was valued at roughly $852 billion as of March 2026 and reported its own run-rate revenue doubling to around $40 billion by August. Model quality leads in this industry have historically lasted months, not years.

None of this means the growth story is fake. The revenue acceleration is real, and it's happening at a scale with genuinely few precedents. It means the risks sit in the parts of the story that are currently the least visible from outside: the actual audited numbers, the compute-contract fine print, and how much of the current growth curve is durable versus front-loaded ahead of an IPO.

## How I'd think about it

Separate two questions, the same way the run-rate number tempts you to merge them.

The first question is: is Anthropic's underlying business growing in a way with real precedent-breaking momentum? On the evidence available, yes — enterprise adoption, Claude Code's trajectory, and the shift toward usage-based, high-value contracts are all real and independently reported.

The second question is: does the $965 billion valuation, or whatever number the IPO eventually prices at, accurately reflect that growth once you account for compute commitments, customer concentration, competitive pressure and regulatory exposure? That question genuinely cannot be answered yet — not by me, not by the analysts publishing $2 trillion valuation forecasts, and not by anyone outside the company until the S-1 is public and the run-rate headline gets replaced by an audited number.

That's the actual thing worth watching once the filing drops: not whether the growth story is impressive — it already is — but whether the audited numbers underneath it look anything like the run-rate one.
